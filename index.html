Physics Rays
Reading the image

Every pixel is a photon run backwards through curved spacetime.

For each pixel, the shader launches a light ray from the camera and steps it along its geodesic, the path light actually takes when gravity bends space. The equation is one line: for Schwarzschild spacetime the orbit obeys u″ + u = 3⁄2 u², with u = rₛ / r. Where the ray lands decides the color: the disk, the horizon, or a star far away.

THE SHADOW

Bigger than the horizon

The black region has a radius of 2.6 rₛ, not 1 rₛ. Any ray aimed closer than b = (3√3⁄2) rₛ to the center gets captured, so the horizon casts a shadow about 2.6 times its own size.

THE FAR SIDE

The disk behind the hole

The arch over the top is the back half of the disk. Its light climbs over the hole and bends toward you. The fainter band under the shadow is the underside of that same far half.

THE PHOTON RING

Light that went around

The thin bright line at the shadow's edge is light that circled the hole once or more near r = 1.5 rₛ, where photons can orbit. Each extra loop makes a ring about eπ ≈ 23 times thinner.

DOPPLER BEAMING

One side is brighter

The inner disk moves at about half the speed of light. Gas coming toward you is blueshifted and beamed brighter. Gas moving away is redshifted and dimmed. Turn it off to see how lopsided it really is.

LENSING

The sky gets folded

Stars near the shadow are smeared into arcs, and the whole sky appears again in thinner rings closer to the edge. A coordinate grid on the sky shows how far the folding goes.

Seen from above

The knife edge at b = 2.598 rₛ

The same equation, solved for a fan of parallel rays arriving from the left. The rays escape or fall in depending only on their impact parameter b. Near the critical value they wind around the photon sphere before deciding. Move the slider toward 2.598 and watch the deflection grow without limit.

At b = 2.598 rₛ a ray would circle forever on the photon sphere, which is unstable. A difference in the tenth decimal place of b decides whether light escapes or falls in. That sensitivity is why the photon ring in the render above is so thin and so bright.

What's modeled and what isn't

Physically exact

Ray paths come from the Schwarzschild orbit equation. Camera directions are corrected into the local frame of a hovering or radially falling observer, including aberration. Disk light gets the special-relativistic Doppler factor from Keplerian orbital speed plus gravitational redshift; since a blackbody stays a blackbody under a frequency shift, the disk color is Planck's law at the shifted temperature.

Simplified

The hole doesn't spin. Real ones do, and spin (the Kerr metric) makes the shadow slightly lopsided. The disk is geometrically thin with a Novikov–Thorne temperature profile and procedural texture. Sgr A* and M87* actually feed on hot, thick, faint flows, and real disks around stellar black holes glow in X-rays at millions of kelvin. The temperature slider is for your eyes.

Light travel time

The render shows the disk as it is "now" everywhere, ignoring the extra time light takes on longer paths. The texture moves at the Keplerian rate for each radius, so you can see differential rotation shearing the gas into spirals.
